Delivery approach

Lot 2 · 26516L02 · €110,000 · 24 months

The science keeps publishing.

Lot 2 is the operational lot: development and technical maintenance of the corporate site at mercator-ocean.eu and of the staff intranet, over 24 months. Institutions notice maintenance only when it fails. Our job is that nobody at Mercator Ocean ever has to think about either system.

Corporate site · development

Every change is rehearsed.

Six working practices for the WordPress estate, applied from week one. None of them is exotic; all of them are missing more often than not.

  • Staging environment. Every evolution, from a new template to a plugin change, is rehearsed on a staging copy before it touches production. The live site never sees an experiment.
  • Versioned deployments. Themes, configuration and build tooling live in version control. Releases are tagged, deployments are repeatable, rollback is one command.
  • Performance budgets, enforced before merge. The Lot 1 audit measured 31 stylesheets and zero WebP images on the current homepage. Budgets in the deployment pipeline, covering request count, page weight and vitals, stop those regressions from creeping back once fixed.
  • Security patching cadence. WordPress core and plugin updates applied on a fixed schedule, tested on staging first; critical advisories are patched out of cycle.
  • Uptime monitoring. Independent monitoring of the corporate site and the intranet, alerting a human when something degrades, not only when it dies.
  • Editorial support. Hands-on help for the team that publishes: new blocks and templates, training, and the small recurring content jobs that never justify a project of their own.

Maintenance

The cadence, in writing.

Maintenance promises are only real when they are scheduled. These four commitments go into the contract, not into a slide.

Precise response windows and severity definitions are agreed at contract start and reported against every month.
CommitmentCadence
Incident responseSame business day: acknowledged, diagnosed and worked until resolved or escalated.
Security patchesApplied within an agreed window after release, staging first; critical advisories out of cycle.
Maintenance reportMonthly, in writing: what changed, what broke, what it cost, what comes next.
Roadmap reviewQuarterly, together with the editorial and IT teams; the backlog is re-sequenced against the institution's calendar.

Support runs on the Toulouse clock. Belgium and Toulouse share the same timezone, so our working day is your working day: no offshore relay, no overnight ticket queue.

Governance

You always own the estate.

A maintenance contract should never become a dependency. Four rules keep the engagement inspectable and reversible for its whole 24 months.

  • A shared backlog. All work lives in a tracker both sides can read and write. Nothing gets built that was not visible first.
  • A monthly written report. Work delivered, incidents and their resolution, and hours consumed against the budget, every month, unprompted.
  • A quarterly review. The roadmap is re-examined each quarter against the institution's own calendar of campaigns, events and releases.
  • Exit documentation. Code, content, credentials and runbooks are documented, kept current and handed over as standard. The institution always owns its estate; leaving us must always be easy. That is what keeps us good.
