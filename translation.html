Ocean Intelligence · European Digital Twin of the Ocean

Digital ocean knowledge, accessible to all

Mercator Ocean International co-develops the European Digital Twin of the Ocean (EDITO) on behalf of the European Commission. Its capability layers answer three questions about the ocean: what now, what next and what if.

The organisation also operates the Copernicus Marine Service for the European Union. The service provides free, open-access ocean data and counts more than 1.5 million users worldwide, with 85,000 subscribers as of 2024.

Preview of the mechanism only. On this page, English lives in the markup and the French is a hand-checked dictionary in the page script. Note the localisation detail: 1.5 million becomes 1,5 million and 85,000 becomes 85 000. In production nothing is hand-written; every French page is produced by the pipeline described below.

Locked terminology

Six terms that must never drift.

An automatic system is only trustworthy on a scientific site when its vocabulary is locked before the first sentence is translated. An extract from the working glossary:

Extract from the locked glossary: English term, French term, and why the pair is locked.
EnglishFrenchWhy it is locked
sea surface temperature température de surface de la mer This is the variable name behind the SST maps and dataset captions. An unlocked engine drifts between near-identical phrasings; a variable name must be identical everywhere it appears.
reanalysis réanalyse A generic engine can render this as nouvelle analyse, which reads as a second look at something. GLORYS12 is a reanalysis; the term names a product category, not an action.
sea ice glace de mer The tempting rendering is banquise, but banquise means pack ice, a narrower concept. Scientific usage keeps glace de mer for the general variable.
forecast prévision Prédiction is the reflex machine output and the wrong register. Operational oceanography, like meteorology, issues prévisions.
biogeochemical biogéochimique A compound adjective that engines split or hyphenate inconsistently. One form is correct, and the whole corpus must use it every time.
in-situ observations observations in situ The Latin stays untranslated, drops its hyphen in French and moves after the noun: three separate chances for an engine to improvise on one short phrase.

These six pairs are standard oceanographic terminology, shown here to demonstrate the format. The production glossary is proposed and agreed with Mercator Ocean's own scientists, then enforced by the engine on every page.

Production pipeline

How a page becomes French.

The demo above shows the mechanism on one block. Production runs as a pipeline, so no page ever depends on someone remembering to translate it.

  1. Content is published in English

    Editors write and publish exactly as they do today. Lot 3 adds a layer beneath the site; it does not change the authoring workflow.

  2. The engine translates with the glossary enforced

    Machine translation produces the French draft with every locked term applied, so scientific vocabulary cannot drift between pages or between months.

  3. Translation memory stores every approved sentence

    An identical sentence is never translated twice. Recurring boilerplate stays word-for-word stable across the site, and the cost of re-translation falls over the 24 months.

  4. High-visibility pages queue for human review

    The homepage, governance and tender pages receive human sign-off before their French goes live. Long-tail pages publish automatically and enter the monthly quality sample.

  5. Publication emits automatic hreflang pairs

    Every EN/FR pair declares its alternate URL, so search engines serve French readers the French page. The Lot 1 audit measured zero hreflang tags on the live site today; the pipeline makes the fix structural rather than manual.

Ongoing operation

Operated, measured, corrected.

The tender asks for implementation and operation. This is the monthly rhythm across the 24-month term.

  • Monthly glossary maintenance

    New terms are proposed from the month's published content, agreed with your team and locked into the engine. The glossary only grows; agreed terms never drift.

  • Quality sampling on a rotating page set

    Each month a rotating sample of published French pages is re-read and scored, so quality is measured across the whole estate over time, not only on launch week.

  • Coverage reporting

    A monthly report states what share of the estate is available in French, what is queued for human review and what changed. Coverage becomes a number, not an impression.

  • Correction turnaround, same timezone

    A reported mistranslation is corrected and the fix is written back to the translation memory, so it cannot recur. We work from Belgium; Toulouse and we share CET, so same-day means the same day.
